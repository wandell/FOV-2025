### Comments from Bill around June 1, 2026.

1. Are you certain that human V1 contains so many more neurons than macaque V1.  The anatomy from the Australian dude (Harnett?) showed that human cortical neurons are much larger and anatomically more complex (in terms of axon and dendrite branching) than macaque neurons.  There may be more neurons in human V1 anyway, but you don’t cite a source for that, and you can’t make that conclusion simply from relative sizes of human and monkey V1.
3. This sentence under “Motion sensitivity in Area V3A is confusing:

Human V3A is highly responsive to motion, whereas macaque V3A is not strongly motion-selective.

Do you want to talk about sensitivity or selectivity?  They are different.  Neurons/areas can be "highly responsive to motion" and NOT be “strongly motion selective.”  And exactly what does motion selective mean anyway?

3. I enjoyed reading about the clusters in human cortex.  I have not seen it laid out like this in summary format.  I would quibble with at least one aspect of your critique of the Felleman and Van Essen diagram - that the human data suggest a coarse hierarchy, but not a strict hierarchy.

   I would argue that the human data cannot address the Felleman and Van Essen hierarchy proposal, which is based on layer-specific, anatomical fingerprints of feedforward and feedback connections between areas.  We have no such data for the human, so the human data must be regarded as neutral concerning the F&VE proposal.  Apparently you are using “hierarchy” in a different sense than F&VE, which risks confusion.
4. In Fig 13, just for clarity, it would be good to label the LPZ mentioned in the figure legend
5. IMO one of the primary sources of error in the animal adult plasticity field is simple investigator bias.  The guys doing the physiology were quite aware of which animals had been manipulated and how, and the cortical responses can be quite wispy, allowing for bias to influence qualitative assessment of receptive field boundaries, etc.  I felt that this was a weakness in these studies even from the get-go, even before you & Nikos started your work together.

6. In the side note on “how maps develop”, you use the term “functional connectivity” without defining it.  Hopefully you have defined it earlier in the book before getting to this point.

7. I am puzzled (and would like to hear more) about the achiasmia individuals.  Seems like they would have spatial confusion about the location of visual objects unless they perceptually suppress the erroneous half-field at some higher level of the cortex.  What does the pRF in V2, etc, look like?

8. I was surprised that you didn’t tackle the issue of cross-modal plasticity of visual cortex in blind adults who have become expert Braille readers (e.g. the Zohary paper, and others I think).  Do you discount this evidence for adult plasticity?

Nice chapter…and it will be a good talk!
