# Slides

## From Cortical Plasticity to Controlled Systems

- Early view: large-scale **cortical reorganization**
- Newer view: **constrained plasticity**
  - Limited structural change
  - Strong role for inputs and modulation

**Key idea:**  
Observed cortical change ≠ cortical mechanism

## Perineuronal Nets (PNNs): Structural Brakes

- Extracellular matrix around PV cells
- Rich in CSPGs (e.g., aggrecan)

Functions:
- Stabilize synapses
- Limit receptor mobility
- Suppress structural remodeling

**Takeaway:**  
PNNs **lock in** mature circuit structure

## Why PNNs Reduce Plasticity

- Anchor synaptic connections → less rewiring
- Support fast, precise PV inhibition
- Reduce variability in network responses

Result:
- Stable, efficient representations
- Reduced adaptability

**Takeaway:**  
PNNs convert cortex into a **low-variance, stable system**

## Why Removing PNNs Reopens Plasticity

- PV cells remain, but:
  - Inhibition becomes more flexible
  - Synapses become modifiable

- Restores:
  - Receptor mobility
  - Structural plasticity
  - Sensitivity to input

**Key idea:**  
Plasticity mechanisms are **suppressed, not lost**


## PV Interneurons Gate Plasticity

- Maturation of PV inhibition → **critical period opens**
- Strong inhibition → **stabilization and closure**

- Too little inhibition → noisy, unstructured
- Intermediate inhibition → optimal plasticity
- Strong inhibition → restricted plasticity

**Takeaway:**  
Plasticity is **gated by inhibition**, not simply reduced by it

## Why Removing PNNs Reopens Plasticity

- PV cells remain, but:
  - Inhibition becomes more flexible
  - Synapses become modifiable

- Restores:
  - Receptor mobility
  - Structural plasticity
  - Sensitivity to input

**Key idea:**  
Plasticity mechanisms are **suppressed, not lost**
## Why PNNs Reduce Plasticity

- Anchor synaptic connections → less rewiring
- Support fast, precise PV inhibition
- Reduce variability in network responses

Result:
- Stable, efficient representations
- Reduced adaptability

**Takeaway:**  
PNNs convert cortex into a **low-variance, stable system**

## PV vs PNN: Division of Roles

- PV interneurons → **control system**
  - Gain, timing, competition

- PNNs → **stabilization layer**
  - Lock circuit configuration

Analogy:
- PV = control
- PNN = lock

**Takeaway:**  
Plasticity depends on the **state of the system**, not just its components


## Types of “Plasticity”

Not all changes are the same:

- Gain modulation (fast)
- Routing / synchronization
- Synaptic weight change
- Structural rewiring
- Input-driven changes (subcortical)

**Takeaway:**  
Distinguish **mechanism**, not just observation

## Toward a Quantitative Model

Plasticity as a **controlled dynamical system**

Timescales:
- Fast: gain, attention, synchrony
- Intermediate: synaptic weights
- Slow: structure (PNNs, wiring)

PV + PNN roles:
- Set learning rate
- Set stability constraints
- Control transition between regimes

**Takeaway:**  
Plasticity is **regulated, multi-timescale control**

## Summary

- Adult cortex is **stable but not fixed**
- PV inhibition → **gates plasticity**
- PNNs → **stabilize and limit change**
- Plasticity can be **reopened by relaxing constraints**

**Core idea:**  
Cortex is a **controlled adaptive system**, not a freely plastic one
# Notes: Cellular Mechanisms of Plasticity and Their Interpretation

## 1. Shift in the Field: From Cortical Reorganization to Constrained Plasticity

Early interpretations:

* Large-scale **cortical reorganization** following lesions or altered input
* Often attributed to **intracortical rewiring**

More recent view:

* Plasticity is **real but limited**
* Many observed changes reflect:

  * **Subcortical reorganization**
  * **Thalamocortical modulation**
  * **Changes in gain or routing**, not structure

Key conceptual distinction:

> Observed cortical change ≠ cortical mechanism

---

## 2. Inhibitory Control of Plasticity (Hensch Framework)

Takao Hensch

### Core idea:

> The maturation of inhibitory circuitry—especially PV interneurons—**gates the critical period**

### Developmental sequence:

* **Early cortex**

  * Weak inhibition
  * High plasticity (but noisy, unstable)

* **PV maturation**

  * Increased GABAergic inhibition
  * Enables structured, experience-dependent plasticity
  * → **Critical period opens**

* **Further maturation**

  * Strong inhibition
  * Stabilized responses
  * → **Critical period closes**

### Key insight:

* Plasticity requires a **minimum level of inhibition**
* Not simply “less inhibition = more plasticity”

---

## 3. Perineuronal Nets (PNNs): Structural “Brakes”

Key contributors:

* Lamberto Maffei (lineage)
* Tiziano Pizzorusso

### Core idea:

> PNNs stabilize circuits and **limit further plasticity**

### Composition:

* Extracellular matrix rich in:

  * Aggrecan
  * Other CSPGs

### Functional roles:

#### 1. Structural stabilization

* Anchor synapses
* Limit dendritic and axonal remodeling

#### 2. Biochemical constraint

* Restrict receptor mobility (AMPA/NMDA)
* Reduce LTP/LTD flexibility

#### 3. Stabilization of PV phenotype

* Support fast-spiking, precise inhibition
* Increase reliability of circuit timing

#### 4. Molecular inhibition of growth

* Bind factors that suppress sprouting

---

## 4. Why PNNs Reduce Plasticity

> PNNs convert PV circuits into a **stable, low-variance control system**

Effects:

* Increased inhibitory precision
* Reduced synaptic turnover
* Reduced structural flexibility

Result:

* Network becomes **stable and efficient**, but less adaptable

---

## 5. Why Removing PNNs Restores Plasticity

Key insight:

> Plasticity mechanisms remain present in adult cortex—they are **suppressed, not eliminated**

Removing PNNs:

* Does **not remove PV cells**
* But changes their functional regime

### Effects:

#### 1. Increased synaptic flexibility

* Receptor mobility restored
* Structural plasticity re-enabled

#### 2. Loosening of inhibitory constraint

* PV inhibition becomes less rigid
* Greater sensitivity to input changes

#### 3. Shift in network dynamics

* More variability
* Greater responsiveness to experience

---

## 6. Conceptual Summary: PV Cells vs PNNs

* **PV interneurons**
  → Provide **control of circuit dynamics**

* **PNNs**
  → Provide **stabilization (lock-in) of that control**

Analogy:

* PV cells = control system
* PNNs = locking mechanism

---

## 7. Integrated View of Plasticity Constraints

Plasticity is governed by multiple interacting systems:

| Mechanism                     | Role                                |
| ----------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| PV inhibition                 | Gates plasticity (on/off threshold) |
| PNNs (ECM)                    | Stabilize and restrict rewiring     |
| Neuromodulation (e.g., Lynx1) | Regulates plasticity gain           |
| Inputs (subcortical/thalamic) | Shape observed cortical responses   |

---

## 8. Types of “Plasticity” (Important Distinction)

Not all observed changes are the same:

* Gain modulation (fast)
* Synchronization / routing changes
* Synaptic weight changes
* Structural rewiring
* Changes inherited from subcortical inputs

---

## 9. Need for a Quantitative Model

Current limitation:

* Many mechanisms identified
* Few **predictive, falsifiable models**

A useful model should:

* Predict:

  * Magnitude of change
  * Timescale
  * Conditions (attention, inhibition, input)
* Distinguish:

  * Dynamic modulation vs structural change

---

## 10. Toward a Computational Framework

A promising direction:

### Multi-timescale control system

* **Fast variables**

  * Gain, attention, synchronization

* **Intermediate variables**

  * Synaptic efficacy

* **Slow variables**

  * Structural constraints (PNNs, wiring)

### Role of PV/PNNs in this view:

* Set:

  * Learning rate
  * Stability constraints
  * Competition strength
  * Threshold for persistent change

---

## 11. Teaching Summary (Concise)

> Adult cortex is not highly plastic, nor is it fixed.
> It is a **controlled system**:
>
> * PV circuits gate when change is possible
> * PNNs stabilize and limit change
> * Plasticity can be reopened by relaxing these constraints

---

## 12. Key References (Conceptual Anchors)

* Hensch (2005): critical period gating
* Pizzorusso et al. (2002): PNN removal restores plasticity
* Morishita et al. (2010): Lynx1 as molecular brake
* Sorg et al. (2016): PNN synthesis
